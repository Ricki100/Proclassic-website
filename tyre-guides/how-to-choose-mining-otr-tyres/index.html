How to Choose Mining OTR Tyres for Loaders and Dump Trucks

OTR tyres are among the biggest running costs on a mine. Here is how to read the codes, pick the right tread and construction, and make each tyre last.

Proclassic Tyres · Updated 25 October 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer

Choose mining OTR tyres in four steps: confirm the exact size from the sidewall, pick the tread code for the job (E for earthmovers and dump trucks, L for loaders, with 3 for traction, 4 for rock and 5 for extra-deep tread), choose bias or radial construction to suit speed and haul distance, and match the ply rating or load star to your payload. Then keep pressures correct, because inflation is the biggest single factor in OTR tyre life.

What "OTR" means

OTR stands for off-the-road. These are tyres for machines that work on mine sites, quarries and construction sites rather than public roads: wheel loaders, articulated and rigid dump trucks, graders, dozers on wheels and telehandlers. They carry huge loads at low speed on rock, mud and gravel, so they are built very differently from truck tyres.

Step 1: Confirm the size

OTR sizes are written in two main ways:

  • Bias sizes such as 23.5-25: 23.5 inches wide, for a 25-inch rim. The dash means bias (cross-ply) construction.
  • Radial sizes such as 23.5R25 or 1400R24: the R means radial.

Read the full size and any markings from the sidewall of the tyre you are replacing. These are the sizes mines and contractors in Zimbabwe ask us for most:

Step 2: Pick the tread code

OTR tyres carry an industry code that tells you what they are designed for. The letter is the machine type and the number is the tread type.

CodeDesigned forUse it when
E-3Earthmover, traction treadDump trucks on general haul roads and mixed ground
E-4Earthmover, rock treadDump trucks on rocky haul roads where cuts are the main risk
L-3Loader, traction treadLoaders in soil, sand and general earthmoving
L-5Loader, extra-deep rock treadLoaders at the face in quarries and hard-rock mines
L-5SLoader, extra-deep smooth treadVery severe rock and underground work, where a smooth tread resists cutting
G-2Grader, traction treadGraders maintaining haul roads

The deeper the tread (3 to 4 to 5), the more rubber there is to absorb cuts, but the more heat a tyre builds if it travels long distances. That leads to the next choice.

Step 3: Bias or radial?

As a rule of thumb, loaders working short cycles in rock often do well on bias L-5 tyres, while dump trucks hauling longer distances benefit from radials. Never mix bias and radial tyres on the same axle.

Step 4: Match the load rating

Bias OTR tyres show a ply rating, such as 16PR, 20PR or 24PR. Radial OTR tyres usually show stars (one, two or three). Both describe how much load the tyre can carry at a given pressure. Work out the machine's operating weight plus payload, see how it splits between front and rear axles, and choose a rating that covers the heaviest axle with a margin. A loader's front axle carries far more weight than its rear when the bucket is full.

Pressure: the biggest factor in tyre life

Underinflation is the most common cause of early OTR tyre failure. It makes the casing flex and overheat, and it exposes the sidewall to cuts. Overinflation makes tyres hard and more likely to be cut or to impact-break on rock.

  1. Set pressure from the tyre maker's load and inflation table for your machine and load.
  2. Check cold, at the start of each shift.
  3. Inflate large OTR tyres in a safety cage or with a clip-on chuck and remote gauge, standing clear of the sidewall.
  4. Consider nitrogen on large tyres, which reduces pressure loss and the risk of fire from heat.

Site habits that save tyres

  • Keep haul roads and loading areas clean. Spilled rock is the main cause of cuts. A grader and a clean-up loader pay for themselves in tyres.
  • Avoid spinning wheels when the bucket hits the pile. Spin cuts the tread on sharp rock.
  • Watch speed and haul length. Running too fast or too far for the tyre's construction overheats it.
  • Inspect daily for cuts, embedded rock and damaged valves.
  • Keep records of hours and causes of removal, so you buy the right tyre next time.

Ask on WhatsApp
What does L-5 mean on a mining tyre?

L means loader and 5 means an extra-deep rock tread. L-5 tyres are made for loaders working in rock, where cut resistance matters most.

What is the difference between E-3 and E-4?

Both are earthmover tyres for dump trucks. E-3 has a traction tread for general haul roads, while E-4 has a deeper rock tread for rocky roads with a high risk of cuts.

Are radial OTR tyres better than bias?

It depends on the job. Radials run cooler and suit longer hauls and higher speeds. Bias tyres have stiffer, more cut-resistant sidewalls and suit short loading cycles on rough sites.

What size tyre does a 40 tonne dump truck use?

Rigid dump trucks in the 35 to 40 tonne class commonly use 18.00-33. Always confirm the size from the sidewall or the machine's manual.
