Agricultural Tyre Pressure: What Farmers Need to Know

The right pressure gives more grip, less soil damage, lower fuel use and longer tyre life. Here is how to get it right on tractors, trailers and implements.

Proclassic Tyres · Updated 25 October 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

Agricultural tyres run at far lower pressures than road vehicles, and the right figure depends on the tyre size, the load on each tyre and the speed. Find it in the tyre maker's load and inflation table, not on a guess. In general, use the lowest pressure the table allows for field work to improve traction and reduce compaction, add pressure for heavy loads and road transport, check cold with a low-pressure gauge, and set both tyres on an axle the same.

Why tractor tyre pressure matters

A tractor tyre is designed to flex and spread its load. When the pressure is right for the load, the tyre lays a long, wide footprint on the ground. That gives:

  • Better traction and less wheel slip, so more of the engine's power pulls the implement.
  • Less soil compaction, which protects root growth and yields.
  • Lower fuel use, because less energy is lost to slip.
  • Longer tyre life and fewer sidewall cracks and failures.

A common mistake is to pump tractor tyres up like truck tyres. Too much pressure shrinks the footprint, which wastes fuel and compacts the soil on every hectare you work.

Where to find the right pressure

Every tyre maker publishes a load and inflation table for each size. It shows the load a tyre can carry at each pressure and speed. To use it:

  1. Find your tyre size and load index on the sidewall, for example 18.4R38 146A8.
  2. Estimate the load on each tyre: weigh each axle with the implement fitted, or use the tractor maker's figures plus ballast and implement weight.
  3. Divide the axle load by the number of tyres on that axle.
  4. Look up the lowest pressure that carries that load at your working speed.

The tractor's handbook also gives recommended pressures for common set-ups. If you do not have either, ask us and we will help you find the figure for your tyre.

Radial tractor tyres are made to run at low pressures and look slightly bulged at the sidewall when correctly inflated. Do not pump them up just because they look soft. Bias tyres have stiffer sidewalls and usually run at higher pressures than radials of the same size.

Ballast and liquid-filled tyres

Many farmers add weight to rear tyres with water or a liquid ballast to improve traction. If your tyres are liquid-filled:

  • Check pressure with the valve at the top of the wheel, and use a gauge designed for liquid-filled tyres.
  • Remember the liquid adds to the load on the tyre when you read the inflation table.
  • Do not fill more than the recommended level, usually about three-quarters with the valve at the top.

Too much ballast is as bad as too little. It increases compaction and loads the drivetrain.

How to check tractor tyre pressure properly

  1. Check cold, before work, with the tractor parked on level ground.
  2. Use a low-pressure gauge. Ordinary car or truck gauges are not accurate at the low pressures tractor tyres use.
  3. Check both tyres on an axle and set them the same, so the tractor pulls straight.
  4. Fit valve caps to keep out dust and moisture.
  5. Check weekly in the season, and whenever you change implements or ballast.

4WD tractors and pressure

On a four-wheel-drive tractor, changing pressure changes the rolling size of each tyre slightly. Keep front and rear pressures as the table recommends, so the front axle's lead stays within range. Big differences make the tractor "wind up", wasting fuel and wearing tyres. Read more about matched sizes in tractor tyre sizes explained.

Ask on WhatsApp
What pressure should a tractor tyre be?

It depends on the tyre size, the load it carries and the speed. Look it up in the tyre maker's load and inflation table. Radial tractor tyres usually run far lower than car or truck tyres.

Should I lower tyre pressure for ploughing?

Use the lowest pressure the inflation table allows for the load. That gives a longer footprint, more grip and less compaction. Raise it again for road transport.

Why do my radial tractor tyres look flat?

Radial tractor tyres are designed to bulge slightly at the sidewall at the correct pressure. Check with a low-pressure gauge before adding air.

How do I check pressure in a water-filled tyre?

Turn the wheel so the valve is at the top and use a gauge made for liquid-ballasted tyres, so liquid does not enter the gauge.

Can wrong pressure damage tractor tyres?

Yes. Underinflation causes sidewall cracks and tyres slipping on the rim. Overinflation causes centre wear, wheel slip and stone damage.
