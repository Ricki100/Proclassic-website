How to Choose the Right Tyres for Your Delivery Fleet

For a delivery fleet, tyres are a running cost you can control. Here is how to choose sizes and patterns, standardise your fleet and cut cost per kilometre.

Proclassic Tyres · Updated 25 October 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

Choose delivery fleet tyres by vehicle and route: 215/75R17.5 and 235/75R17.5 for light and medium trucks such as Isuzu, Hino and Fuso, 275/70R22.5 for larger distribution trucks, 7.50R16 for tube-type light trucks and 195R15C for kombis and vans. Use regional or urban patterns for town work and mixed-service patterns for rural routes. Standardise sizes and brands across the fleet, track cost per kilometre, and buy through one supplier who can quote every size at once.

Start with what your vehicles run

Walk the yard and record the size on every vehicle, front and rear. Most delivery fleets in Zimbabwe use a handful of sizes:

SizeTypical vehicles
215/75R17.5Light trucks such as Isuzu NPR, Hino 300 and Fuso Canter
235/75R17.5Medium delivery trucks
275/70R22.5Larger distribution trucks and city delivery rigids
295/80R22.5, 315/80R22.5Heavy rigids and horses on regional runs
7.50R16Tube-type light trucks and pick-up conversions
195R15C, 195/70R15CKombis, panel vans and light commercial vehicles

The "C" in 195R15C means commercial: the tyre has a stronger casing and higher load rating than a car tyre of the same size. Never fit car tyres to a loaded van or kombi. See our bus and kombi tyres page for passenger transport sizes.

Match the pattern to the route

Route typeWhat to look for
Town and city deliveryUrban or regional pattern with strong, scuff-resistant sidewalls for kerbs and frequent stops
Intercity runs on tarHighway rib on the steer axle, regional drive pattern for mileage and fuel economy
Rural and farm deliveriesMixed-service pattern with a cut-resistant compound for gravel and dirt roads
Mixed fleetsAll-position regional tyres that can move between axles

If some trucks do town work and others go rural, it is worth running two specifications rather than one compromise across the fleet.

Standardise to save money

A fleet that runs ten sizes and six brands is hard to manage. Standardising brings real savings:

  • Fewer spares in stock, and a spare that fits more vehicles.
  • Better prices, because you buy more of fewer items.
  • Cleaner records, so you can compare tyre life fairly.
  • Easier rotation between vehicles and axles.

When you replace vehicles, consider tyre sizes as part of the decision. A new truck on the same size as the rest of the fleet is cheaper to run.

Measure cost per kilometre, not price per tyre

The cheapest tyre to buy is not always the cheapest to run. Work out:

Cost per km = tyre price (plus fitting) ÷ kilometres run before replacement

Record the fitting date, odometer reading, position and brand for every tyre, then the reading and reason when it comes off. After a few months you will know which tyres give the lowest cost per kilometre on your routes. Many fleets find that a slightly dearer tyre that runs much further is the better buy.

Daily and weekly checks for drivers

  1. Before the first trip: look at every tyre for cuts, bulges, stones between duals and low pressure.
  2. Weekly: check pressures cold with a calibrated gauge and set them for the load.
  3. Every service: measure tread depth, check wheel nuts and look for uneven wear that points to alignment or suspension faults.
  4. Report damage early. A small cut repaired today is cheaper than a blow-out tomorrow.

Delivery trucks often run light in the afternoon and heavy in the morning. Set pressures for the heaviest load of the day.

Replace on time

Set a fleet minimum tread depth, and replace earlier on the steer axle, where a failure is most dangerous. Replace any tyre with a bulge, exposed cords or a sidewall cut, and watch the age of tyres on vehicles that do low mileage. Ask on WhatsApp
What tyre size does an Isuzu NPR use?

Many Isuzu NPR light trucks run 215/75R17.5, but check the sidewall of your vehicle's tyres, as fitments vary by model and year.

What does C mean on a tyre like 195R15C?

C means commercial. The tyre has a stronger casing and higher load rating than a car tyre of the same size, for vans and kombis.

How can a fleet reduce tyre costs?

Standardise sizes and brands, keep pressures correct, match patterns to routes, rotate where allowed, and track cost per kilometre so you buy the tyres that last longest.

Should delivery trucks use the same tyres on all axles?

Not usually. Use a steer pattern on the front axle and a drive pattern on driven axles. Some all-position regional tyres can be used on both.
