How to read a car tyre size
195/65R15 91H

195 = width in mm · 65 = sidewall height as % of width · R = radial · 15 = rim in inches · 91 = load index · H = speed rating.

265/65R17 AT

AT = all-terrain (gravel and dirt as well as tar) · HT = highway terrain (quiet and smooth on tar) · LT/C = light-truck/commercial for heavy loads.

What tyre size does my car need?

The size is moulded on your current tyre’s sidewall, e.g. 195/65R15 91H, and is also listed on a sticker in the driver’s door frame or in the owner’s manual. Send us the size or a photo on WhatsApp and we’ll confirm what fits.

How do I read a car tyre size like 195/65R15?

195 is the width in millimetres, 65 is the sidewall height as a percentage of the width, R means radial and 15 is the rim diameter in inches. The numbers and letter after it (e.g. 91H) are the load index and speed rating — match or exceed your vehicle’s original rating.

When should I replace my tyres?

Replace tyres when the tread gets close to the tread-wear indicator bars in the grooves, or earlier if they are cracked, bulging or damaged. Many experts recommend changing at around 3 mm of tread for safe braking in the rainy season.

Highway (HT) or all-terrain (AT) tyres for my SUV or bakkie?

Highway tyres are quieter, smoother and more economical if you mostly drive on tar. All-terrain tyres have a chunkier tread for gravel, farm roads and mud. If you regularly leave the tar — or drive in the rainy season on dirt roads — choose AT.

Do you fit and balance the tyres?

Yes. We fit, balance and pressure-check new tyres at our Harare and Mutare branches.

Can I replace just two tyres?

Yes — replace tyres in pairs on the same axle so they wear and grip evenly. New tyres are usually fitted to the rear for stability. We’ll advise you at fitment.
